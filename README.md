# Project-Management-PowerBI
A Project Management Dashboard Created using Power BI
- **Objective:** Built an interactive Power BI dashboard to give management a single view of project progress, budget, billing, and risk across 1,836 projects, replacing the need to check multiple reports.
- **What I did:** Cleaned and modeled the project data, created DAX measures (completion %, financial progress %, bill clearance %), and designed KPI cards, donut, bar, area, and funnel charts with Financial/Physical/Progress toggle buttons.
Power BI-Project Management Dashboard-Findings
KPI-Cards
The three KPI cards(top-row), the first one is about total projects. Dashboard has tracked about 1836 projects, and the completed ones equals 37, while about 97.98%, which is a number of 1799 projects are still remaining to get completed. 
The amount awarded for the project is about 3486.54 crore, and about 75.2% of the money is still unspent. Only a quarter has been used, that is about 2621.87 crore.
 The bill amount shows the total value of bills which was raised during the project. About 864.66 crore is approved and 100.70 crore is pending, which makes about 10% of amount is  remaining.

Risk Resolution Progress 
•	There are 36 risks in total: 32 (88.89%) are still unresolved and 4 (11.11%) are resolved
Risk Analysis 
•	Natural risks are shown as the largest cause. Contract-related risks come second.
Budget Allocation 
•	This counts projects by size of budget
•	About 69% of projects are under 1 Cr, so most are small. Only 34 projects (18 + 16) are above 25 Cr.
•	The10–25 Cr group (129) is larger than the 5–10 Cr group (101).

Awarded Amount vs Expenditure by City 
•	The blue line is the awarded amount and the dark blue line is the expenditure
•	Westminster has the highest award (466.23 Cr) but has spent only 73 Cr, about 16%.
•	Oxford is the best at using its money: 158 Cr spent out of 317.55 Cr, about 50%.
•	Bristol also spends well: 81 of 184.43 Cr, about 44%.
•	Camden (36 of 237.50 Cr, about 15%) and Liverpool (28 of 164.67 Cr, about 17%) are among the weakest.
•	Manchester has the smallest award (134.05 Cr).

Funnel Chart 
•	Only 20.4% of CPR-approved projects have been completed .
•	The biggest drop is between Awarded (1,308) and Started (711). About 600 projects have been awarded but have not begun work, so this is the main bottleneck.
•	There is another large drop from AA Issued (1,834) to TA Issued (1,434), about 400 projects.
•	AA Issued (1,834) is slightly higher than CPR Approved (1,796).


Pending Category & Milestone Category
•	More than half of all projects (963 of 1,836 about 52%) have not started.

Financial, Physical and Progress
Financial Progress (how much money is used)
•	About 65% of projects have used less than a quarter of their budget.
•	The pattern is split: most projects are barely started, and a good number are almost finished (488).
•	Very few are in the middle (50–75% and 25–50%).
Physical Progress (how much actual work is done)
•	The physical and financial progress shows equal progress while progress difference tells a different case.
File 3: Progress Difference (difference between financial and physical progress)
•	About 62% of projects have almost no gap between money spent and work done.
•	Projects with a large gap (50% or more) could signal overspending or delays.

Case-Summary
•	There are 1,836 projects worth 3,486.54 Cr, and only about 25% of the money has been spent.
•	Many projects are stuck before starting. 963 have not started and about 600 are awarded but not begun.
•	Almost 89% of risks are still unresolved. Natural and contract risks are the biggest.
•	Oxford and Bristol use their money best. Westminster, Camden and Liverpool use theirs slowly.
•	Only about 10% of bills are pending.
•	Most projects are small under 1 Cr Budget.
